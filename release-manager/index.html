THE PLAYER HANDBOOK

Your first day. Your decisions.

Pick your character, walk into MEGACORP and build a party of three developers. Your three hired developers appear in named seats around the table. Click a developer to hear about their actual current task, queue, completed work and capacity warnings. Their conversation links straight to the relevant task on the board. Click people around the table: your character walks over, then the conversation opens. Click someone else to change destination. The clock pauses while walking. Reduced-motion settings skip the walk. Meet the developers explains each person’s skills, costs and pace. Green labels identify a specialist match, with a tap-to-open explanation. Use the project board to inspect evidence, choose a priority and assign work with buttons. In stakeholder conversations, keys 1–3 choose a response. Escape returns to the room. In character selection, left and right arrows change character. Full screen and sound are optional. Choose at-your-pace play for no clock.

You manage a fictional product through six releases. Pick exactly three developers within 12 hiring credits each release. Specialists get +1 work per day on matching tasks. Team energy and technical debt carry forward.

  1. Plan: each card has evidence, effort and a stakeholder. Choose a developer and a priority. One developer works on one task per day. In MoSCoW, they start with the highest priority; equal priorities follow card order. Won’t means no work this release. In RICE, they start with the highest-scoring included task; equal scores follow card order. Choose Include or Defer on each ticket.
  2. Choose a method: MoSCoW means Must (release cannot work without it), Should (important, workaround exists), Could (nice if capacity permits), Won’t (explicitly deferred this time). RICE = reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort. All estimates here share the same release horizon. RICE ranks tickets and developer queues by score. It does not override release requirements. Your selected method is saved with your career.
  3. Run: start the clock or use untimed mode. Every 12 seconds is a working day. Stakeholder decisions stop time. Pause whenever you need. Switching developers keeps completed work.
  4. Release: at day six, ship or negotiate one extra day. Read the personal release review: it explains your priorities, skill matches, queues and stakeholder choices, with a change to try next time. Keep playing. Low meters reduce capacity but never lock you out of learning.

Points: 10 × shipped value, minus 100 per missed release requirement and 20 per unfinished MoSCoW Must. Customer trust, leadership support and team health tell a wider story than points. There is no reward for clicking quickly.

Carry-over: energy below 35 or debt above 55 each reduce daily work by 1 (minimum 1). Reviews and quality work reduce debt. Mentoring Sam adds 1 to Sam’s future daily capacity. Later consequences appear in the activity log.

Progress autosaves on this device when browser storage is available. Reset removes this game’s save. Download field notes to keep your decisions. The app stores its files on your device for offline play after the first successful load. Saved careers stay in this browser; there is no cross-device sync. To remove offline files, clear this site’s browser data. Sound is optional and generated locally. No analytics or third-party requests.
