Flight manual / the method, controls & privacy

Restore the systems. Get the fleet home.

You lead the Curiosity’s tech crew. Three system cores power the jump home. Explore the bridge and work its consoles to recover them.

  1. Docking: build a two-gate validation circuit and send real JavaScript test inputs through it. Swap modules when a valid pilot gets stranded or an invalid ID slips through.
  2. Navigation: dispatch two repair drones, launch pilot fleets and compare completed bookings with attempts and completion rates.
  3. Delivery: schedule jobs within the crew’s shift, rehearse a release and agree a new plan when testing reveals more work.

Click a console or use its named button to walk there and open it automatically. With the bridge focused, use WASD or arrow keys and press E nearby. Native buttons provide the full experience with keyboard or touch. Escape closes a console or pauses the bridge. No countdown or sound. Reduced-motion preferences stop decorative animation and make automatic walking immediate.

Experiments are free, and you can repair and retry. There are no quiz questions or scored answers. Each restored core records your experiments in a downloadable flight log. The drone gains, capacity estimates and ship meters are fixed fictional teaching models, not forecasts or measures of real people.

The underlying method is OODA: Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. Inspect what happens, understand the evidence, try a plan, then check its effects and adapt. This is a simplified civilian adaptation of the military decision-making framework associated with John Boyd.

No account, analytics, cookies or saved answers. Progress stays in memory and resets on reload. Downloads are made on your device. Fonts are built-in monospace; the original generated bridge image is shipped locally, with code-drawn characters and overlays. Hosting handles ordinary page requests.
