Make a decision. See what changes. Go round again.

OODA stands for Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. It is a military decision-making framework associated with John Boyd. Here it is adapted into a simplified civilian learning game. Real decisions can move back and forth between these activities.

  1. Observe: visit all three stations and gather evidence.
  2. Orient: separate recorded facts, assumptions and unknowns. In this game, an assumption is an explanation someone is treating as true; an unknown has not been established.
  3. Decide: select an action based on what you know.
  4. Act: inspect the outcome and respond to fresh evidence.

Use arrow keys or WASD while the bridge is focused. Press E near a station. The suggested next station has a numbered highlight; you can investigate in any order. Click or tap a station on the map, use its named button below, or follow the next-step prompt. Clicking a station walks you there and opens its clue automatically. Each clue opens in a readable window; close it or use its suggested next action to continue. Every mission is playable using buttons alone. Pause stops movement. Escape pauses or closes the guide. There is no countdown, sound or flashing effect. Movement respects your reduced-motion preference.

Learning points reward completing the evidence check, choosing a useful action and adapting to new evidence. You can correct classifications without losing points. Service health, crew energy and pilot trust are fictional, bounded teaching indicators, not real measurements. Every choice allows you to finish and learn.

No account, analytics, cookies or saved answers. Progress stays in memory and resets on reload. Field notes download on your device. Fonts are the browser’s built-in monospace family. Hosting handles ordinary page requests. The original spaceship environment was generated for this game; characters and overlays are drawn in code.
