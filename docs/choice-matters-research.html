Coded Female / research reviewed 10 November 2026

Choice Matters

Redesign confusing screens. Make pricing clear, remove the pressure and put people in control.

What the evidence supports

Four comparable games were reviewed using their own descriptions and direct player accounts where available. Public comments are individual experiences, not a representative survey. A missing feature below is an opportunity inferred from the reviewed material, rather than proof that no competitor offers it.

What changed

  • A clear identity: Choice Matters and the agreed tagline replace the previous name on the game, arcade card, related-game recommendation and practice-shop return link. The original URL remains usable.
  • Eight cases: retain the five Loopbox repairs and add optional permissions, trial renewal and sponsored recommendations. Each has an original fictional situation and a practical review prompt.
  • Specific feedback: explain each setting separately so a partially correct design receives useful guidance. Show the underlying pattern after a successful repair.
  • Comparison and replay: switch between the original screen and the current design. Guided play offers hints immediately; the mixed casebook shuffles cases and options and unlocks hints after the first check.
  • Transfer to work: compare evidence checks, record actual attempts and write a review plan. Notes include a checklist, worked example and the learner’s reflection.
  • Continuity: optional tab-only checkpoints, direct case navigation and a restart confirmation. Reflections and shop details are excluded from saved progress.
  • Preserved practical demo: try the existing Loopbox order, optional extras, account and cancellation journey. It keeps its state when returning to the case list during the same visit.

Learning guidance and originality

The OECD’s Dark commercial patterns report and Deceptive Patterns taxonomy support the topics and pattern vocabulary. All new scenarios, settings, review prompts and screen illustrations were authored for Coded Female. No competitor screens, puzzles or artwork were copied.

The game does not claim real visitor research, conversion improvements, legal compliance or a professional qualification. Its review prompts suggest what to check with people; they do not substitute for doing that research.

Validation and remaining limits

The repository suite passes 203 tests, including all 47 design-setting combinations and malformed checkpoint handling. Browser checks completed both modes, all eight cases, retry and hint paths, all 24 evidence responses, comparison, partial and complete resume, opt-out, reset cancellation, reflection readback and the shop’s order and cancellation flow. Forty-eight case layouts at 320, 390, 760, 850, 851 and 1280px had no horizontal page overflow.

The field-note text and ready-to-save download response were checked. An actual downloaded file was not independently recovered. Tests use browser viewports; physical phones, Safari, assistive technology and learning effectiveness with actual players remain untested.

Next useful validation: observe a small group from the intended audience completing a repair and explaining its purpose, then ask them to plan a check for an unfamiliar screen. Use their sticking points to decide what to change next. Competitiveness and learning gains are hypotheses until tested with players.
